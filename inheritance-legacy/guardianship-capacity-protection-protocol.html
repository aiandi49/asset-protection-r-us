The four habits that kept a wheelchair from ever becoming legal proof of anything

Disability is not incapacity

Article I

Purpose

This model addresses a specific and serious form of financial abuse: using a physical disability or a difficult medical recovery as pretext to pursue a legal finding of incapacity, with the actual goal of gaining unrestricted access to someone's assets — often timed to occur before a divorce settlement would otherwise protect those assets from exactly that kind of claim.

Article II

Physical disability is not legal incapacity

Guardianship and conservatorship law concerns a person's cognitive and executive decision-making capacity — the ability to understand information, weigh consequences, and communicate a decision. It does not concern mobility, physical independence, or a person's ability to perform manual tasks. A wheelchair, a period of depression following a catastrophic injury, or a slower physical recovery are not, on their own, evidence of the kind of impairment guardianship law is designed to address. Petitions that rely on this conflation are built on a legally false premise from the start.

Article III

What actually establishes incapacity

Most states require clear and convincing evidence of incapacity before a guardianship or conservatorship can be imposed — a meaningfully high evidentiary standard. That evidence must generally come from an independent clinical or neuropsychological evaluation conducted by a qualified professional with no financial or personal stake in the outcome. A single family member's sworn declaration, however detailed, is not itself sufficient and is routinely treated by courts as exactly what it is: testimony from an interested party, not a medical finding.

In the Bellamy case, the independent evaluation obtained weeks earlier documented full cognitive function and intact executive decision-making — conducted by a medical board with no connection to either side. That single document dissolved the entire petition before it could be filed.

Article IV

Getting ahead of it: the baseline assessment

Following any major medical event that could plausibly be mischaracterized later — a spinal injury, a stroke, a significant surgery, a period of documented depression — obtaining an independent cognitive and neurological evaluation and filing it directly with your attorney creates a dated, professional baseline that any future incapacity claim has to contend with. This is not paranoia; it is the same logic as any other form of documentation built before it's needed rather than scrambled together after a crisis begins.

Article V

The "building a paper trail" pattern

A recognizable pattern in these situations involves someone quietly documenting a person's minor decisions during a genuinely vulnerable period — financial choices made while medicated, decisions made during acute grief or depression — specifically to reframe them later as evidence of a broader pattern of impaired judgment. Keeping your own contemporaneous notes about significant decisions and the reasoning behind them during any recovery period is a direct counter to this tactic, since it establishes your own account of your reasoning at the time rather than leaving that account to be constructed by someone else afterward.

Watch for: a family member unusually interested in cataloging your decisions during a medical recovery · language shifting from concern about your wellbeing to language about who should "make decisions on your behalf" · a guardianship or conservatorship petition surfacing in close proximity to a divorce filing or major asset event.

Article VI

A durable power of attorney that actually protects you

A properly drafted durable power of attorney intended to take effect only upon genuine incapacity — a "springing" power of attorney — should require written certification from two independent, unaffiliated treating physicians before it activates, not the judgment of a single family member or the person who would benefit from it taking effect. The named agent should be someone without a competing financial interest in the outcome. This structure ensures that if incapacity ever does become real, the response is triggered by medical fact, not by whoever moves first.

Article VII

Consequences for a fraudulent petition

Filing a guardianship or conservatorship petition based on fabricated, exaggerated, or discriminatory grounds carries real legal exposure for the person who files it — most states allow the target of a fraudulent petition to countersue for fraud, perjury, and in some cases conspiracy, particularly where sworn declarations are later shown to be knowingly false. Document every step of a suspected fraudulent attempt in writing and involve counsel immediately rather than waiting for a petition to actually be filed.

Article VIII

Limitations & governing law

This model is general guidance and is not a substitute for individualized legal advice. Guardianship and conservatorship standards, the evidentiary threshold for incapacity, and the requirements for a valid durable power of attorney all vary by state. A licensed elder law or disability law attorney should be consulted directly regarding any real capacity concern or suspected guardianship abuse.
