Article I

Purpose

This model offers a second foundation archetype, distinct from a foundation built around general social-service giving: one built to fund early-career professionals in a specific field, named to honor a parent, mentor, or founder — using their name to keep giving the exact kind of help they once could have used, to the kind of person they once were.

Article II

Choosing a narrow, evaluable mandate

A mandate defined in concrete, checkable terms is both easier to grant against fairly and more durable over time than a broad one. Define exactly who qualifies: a technical or professional demonstration requirement, the specific capital gap being filled, and a defined support window — commonly framed as bridging someone's first two to three years of independent practice, rather than open-ended ongoing support.

"Fund independent engineers and architects in their early stages who've demonstrated the technical foundation but lack the capital to get past the first three years without attaching themselves to a larger organization that will eventually own them" is a mandate a grant committee can actually evaluate against, applicant by applicant.

Article III

Funding it with capital that already did its job

A meaningful funding source for a memorial foundation of this kind is capital that was once put at genuine risk on the founder's behalf and later returned — an emergency loan that was formally recognized and repaid, proceeds from a settlement, or funds recovered once a dispute resolved favorably. Money that already proved its purpose once, protecting or rebuilding something real, carries a natural continuity into funding the next generation of people trying to build something of their own.

Article IV

Structuring it like any other private foundation

The legal mechanics of forming this foundation are identical to any other private foundation, regardless of mission: incorporate a nonprofit corporation, assemble a board of at least three directors, draft bylaws and a mission statement defining the mandate precisely, file IRS Form 1023 for 501(c)(3) recognition, obtain an EIN, and fund it only after 501(c)(3) status is approved so contributions qualify for charitable tax treatment from day one. What changes based on mission is the granting criteria and evaluation process, addressed next — not the formation steps themselves.

Article V

Building an evaluation process for a narrow mandate

A grant committee for a field-specific foundation should include at least one or two people who can genuinely assess technical or professional competence in that field — not rely on the founder's judgment alone, which risks becoming inconsistent or overly personal over time. Clear, written criteria, a defined support period per recipient, and some means of tracking whether recipients actually reach independent viability all matter for the foundation to function as intended rather than drift.

  • A technical review step for each applicant, conducted by someone qualified to judge the specific craft or field
  • A defined funding period per recipient, with clear terms for renewal or graduation out of support
  • A simple, honest tracking mechanism — even informal check-ins — for whether recipients are actually building toward independence

Article VI

Naming it well

Naming a foundation after a parent, mentor, or founder is a way of making sure their name stays attached permanently to exactly the kind of help they would have wanted to give, in the field they actually worked in. Over time, the mission itself becomes part of how that name is remembered — not just the fact that a foundation exists, but what it specifically does and for whom.

Article VII

Limitations & governing law

This model is general guidance and is not a substitute for individualized legal advice. Private foundation formation, excise tax treatment, and minimum distribution requirements are governed by federal tax law and do not vary by mission, but state-level nonprofit registration requirements do vary. An attorney and CPA should structure any real foundation of this kind.
