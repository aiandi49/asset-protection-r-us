Article I

Purpose

This model is offered as a plain-language guide for what to have in place before a closing hits an unexpected title or documentation defect that freezes funds in escrow. It is built around a common, survivable pattern: a technical gap in an old corporate record, a short cure window, and the temptation of "rescue" capital that costs far more than its stated price.

Article II

The defect and its cure

A chain-of-title gap most often traces back to an old corporate restructuring where a transfer was executed correctly but a required certificate was prepared and never filed with the state — frequently because the attorney handling it left before the final administrative step was completed. The legal right usually still exists; the paper trail simply has a hole in it.

  • Locate the original attorney or filing agent who handled the historical transfer
  • Obtain a sworn statement confirming the intent and execution of the original transfer
  • Submit the corrected certification to the state on an expedited basis
  • Deliver the bank or escrow agent's counsel a complete, re-assembled chain of title, not just the missing piece
In the Ironwood case, the underlying patent transfer was never in dispute — only the filing was missing. The entire 48-hour crisis was a documentation gap, not a defect in the deal itself. Most title freezes are.

Article III

Guarantor compensation

Where a bank or escrow agent requires a financial guarantor to stand behind the account during a cure window, that guarantor should be compensated in cash, at a rate that reflects the actual risk and the actual length of the window — nothing more, and critically, nothing else. A guarantee is a short-term, well-defined risk. It should be priced like one.

Article IV

No operational rights

The guarantor holds no board seat, no voting interest, no management role, and no security interest beyond the specific escrow funds being guaranteed. The guarantee is a financial instrument, not a foothold. If a counterparty's term sheet quietly bundles operational rights into what is presented as a financing accommodation, that is the term sheet to decline.

Article V

Automatic dissolution

The guarantee terminates immediately and irrevocably upon confirmed release of the escrowed funds at closing. No further consent, negotiation, or notice period is required from either party. The cleanest guarantees are the ones that are hardest to remember six months later, because they simply ended on schedule.

Article VI

The red-flag test for predatory bridge offers

A recurring pattern in distressed and time-pressured closings is an outside party offering to solve the immediate liquidity problem in exchange for equity, control, or a seat at the table once the crisis passes. The offer is framed as generosity extended under difficult circumstances. It is worth testing any such offer against the following:

Watch for: a guarantee conditioned on receiving equity or "controlling interest" upon closing · financing tied to board representation or veto rights · urgency language used to discourage comparing terms elsewhere · a counterparty who already has informal knowledge of your situation before you've briefed them · a structure that survives and expands after the emergency it was meant to solve is over.

A guarantee that only helps if it also transfers control was never priced as a guarantee. It was priced as an acquisition, discounted for the appearance of a favor.

Article VII

Standing diligence, going forward

Chain-of-title and intellectual-property ownership should be part of the standard diligence checklist on every acquisition, not something reviewed only when a closing is already underway. A corporate history that includes prior distress, multiple restructurings, or entities that were merged or dissolved deserves a dedicated title review well before a purchase agreement is signed — not during the 48 hours before closing.

Article VIII

Limitations & governing law

This model is general guidance and is not a substitute for individualized legal advice. Escrow, title-cure, and guarantee mechanics vary by state, by lender, and by the specific purchase agreement's default and cure provisions. A licensed corporate or M&A attorney should structure any real bridge guarantee or title-cure filing before funds are at risk.

Article IX

Acknowledgment

This guide is intended for general education, inspired by a dramatized story. Anyone facing a real escrow freeze or considering a real bridge guarantee should do so with the assistance of their own attorney and their own corporate counsel, before signing anything.
