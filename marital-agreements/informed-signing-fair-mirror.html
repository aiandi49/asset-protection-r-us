Key terms, at a glance

The four provisions that turn disclosure into understanding

Plain-language summary

A one-page version, in real words

Attached to the full agreement is a plain-language summary — what it means in practice, not just in legal terms — initialed separately from the main signature.

Independent counsel

Required, not just offered

Both parties must meet with their own attorney, apart from the other side's, and confirm that meeting happened before the agreement can be signed.

Waiting period

No signing on the same day it's presented

A minimum review window separates the day the agreement is presented from the earliest day it can be signed — no rushed table, no "just a formality."

Reciprocal protection

The same shield, on both sides

Premarital holdings are protected for whichever spouse brought them in — and future ventures either spouse builds during the marriage get the same treatment, not just his.

Article I

Purpose and intent

This agreement is offered as a model counterpart to a prenuptial agreement previously signed by Vanessa Carter without a full understanding of its terms. Where that agreement disclosed everything on paper but explained nothing aloud, this version treats disclosure and understanding as two separate requirements — both necessary before a signature means anything.

Article II

Full financial disclosure

Both parties exchange a complete, itemized net worth statement, including corporate holdings, equity, intellectual property, trust structures, and real property, regardless of whether either party expects the other to ask about it. This statement is attached as an exhibit and reviewed line by line with each party's own counsel before any waiver is signed.

Article III

The right to understand

A plain-language summary of this agreement — no more than two pages, written for a reader with no legal background — is attached and initialed separately from the full document. Each party confirms in writing that they met individually with their own attorney, that their questions were answered, and that a minimum of seven days passed between first receiving this agreement and signing it.

This is the article the original signing skipped. It wasn't missing information — it was missing time. A document handed over as "just a formality" and signed within minutes isn't informed consent, even when every clause in it is completely legal.

Article IV

Separate property

Premarital assets — including corporate holdings, equity, intellectual property, business interests, and trust-held property — remain the sole property of whichever spouse held them before the marriage, along with any future appreciation. This protection applies equally, regardless of which spouse's premarital holdings are larger.

Article V

Marital residence

Where a residence is held in a trust or entity predating the marriage, that fact is disclosed clearly, in the plain-language summary described in Article III, before either party moves in — not discovered for the first time in a divorce filing years later.

Article VI

Personal income and future ventures

Each party retains their own personal income and earnings during the marriage. Any business, investment, or venture either spouse builds during the marriage — not just the higher-earning spouse — is treated under the same separate-property standard described in Article IV.

Article VII

Spousal support

Neither party waives the right to seek spousal support. Should the marriage end, either spouse may request support, calculated according to the length of the marriage and each party's financial standing and contributions to the household.

Article VIII

Amendment, severability & governing law

This agreement may be amended only by a written instrument signed by both parties. Should any provision be held unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force. This agreement shall be governed by the laws of the state in which it is executed, with disputes subject to neutral, mutually agreed arbitration.

Article IX

Acknowledgment

Each party signs a separate statement affirming: "I have read this agreement, had it explained to me by my own independent counsel, understood the plain-language summary, and had adequate time to consider it before signing." This model is offered as the standard every prenuptial agreement should meet, regardless of the wealth gap between the parties.

Signatures

This sample is unsigned and non-binding. A real prenuptial agreement should be signed only after independent review by each party's own attorney.
