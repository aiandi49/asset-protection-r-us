Key terms, at a glance

The four provisions that make an agreement actually fair

Mutual disclosure

Both spouses show their full hand first

Every personal, business, and trust-held asset is itemized and verified by both parties' independent counsel — before anyone is asked to waive a claim to anything.

Reciprocal waivers

Whatever one side signs away, so does the other

No waiver of claims runs in only one direction. If a spouse gives something up, the same standard applies to their partner.

Clear trust boundaries

Trust property is named, not assumed

Assets held in a trust or business entity are identified in a written schedule for both spouses, so no one mistakes someone else's property for shared property.

Fair consideration

No favors offered in the dark

If one spouse takes on a financial obligation for the other's family, the agreement states plainly what they receive in return — reviewed by their own attorney, not assumed.

Article I

Purpose and intent

This agreement is offered as a model counterpart to an earlier postnuptial agreement presented to Ethan Sterling by the Croft family. Where that document asked one spouse to waive her claims without ever describing what either side actually owned, this version starts from the opposite premise: neither spouse should be asked to sign away a claim to something that has never been named, valued, or shown to them.

Article II

Mutual disclosure of assets

Both parties shall provide a complete, itemized disclosure of their personal, business, and trust-held assets, verified by each party's own independent counsel, before any waiver of claims is executed. This disclosure shall include the legal ownership structure of the marital residence, vehicles, art, jewelry, and any property held through a trust or business entity.

This is the article the original agreement skipped entirely. Isabella was asked to waive her claim to "Ethan Sterling's assets" without either side ever writing down what those assets were. A fair agreement starts with the list, not the waiver.

Article III

Separate and trust property

Property held in a trust, LLC, or other business entity by either spouse — whether acquired before or during the marriage — is identified by name in an attached schedule and expressly excluded from marital property. This schedule is prepared and reviewed jointly, so that neither spouse later discovers, only in the event of a separation, what the other legally owned all along.

Article IV

Reciprocal waiver of claims

Any waiver of claims to a spouse's separate property applies equally to both parties. Where one spouse waives a claim to the other's personal or business assets, the same waiver, on the same terms, is granted in return. No agreement under this model may bind only one party.

Article V

Marital property and shared assets

Assets acquired jointly during the marriage, through the effort or resources of either spouse, are presumed to be shared equally absent clear evidence to the contrary. This presumption applies without regard to which spouse's name appears on an account or title.

Article VI

Spousal support

Neither party waives the right to seek spousal support. Should the marriage end, either spouse may request support, calculated according to the length of the marriage and each party's financial standing and contributions, financial and otherwise, to the household.

Article VII

Consideration for family obligations

Where one spouse assumes a financial obligation — such as guaranteeing a loan — on behalf of the other spouse's family, this agreement states plainly what that spouse receives in exchange. No such obligation shall be structured as a one-way favor or hidden within a broader waiver of claims.

Article VIII

Amendment, severability & governing law

This agreement may be amended only by a written instrument signed by both parties. Should any provision be held unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force. This agreement shall be governed by the laws of the state in which it is executed, with disputes subject to neutral, mutually agreed arbitration.

Article IX

Acknowledgment

Each party acknowledges that they have had the opportunity to review this agreement, that they enter into it freely and without duress, and that they have been advised to seek independent legal counsel prior to signing. This model is offered in good faith as the standard a postnuptial agreement should meet, regardless of which spouse initiates it.

Signatures

This sample is unsigned and non-binding. A real postnuptial agreement should be signed only after independent review by each party's own attorney.
